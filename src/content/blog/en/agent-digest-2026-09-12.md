## The Pattern

Today's three strongest signals come from three different repositories yet answer the same question: **who owns long-lived state, and when may it change hands**.

Cloudflare agents@0.23.0 answers 'hand over once, never look back'. ai-chat 0.12 imports the whole message table into a linear Sessions chain on the object's first wake, drops the old table only after every row imported, and a rollback leaves an empty conversation; think 0.18 turns withCachedPrompt() into a no-op because 'the frozen prompt is always persisted now'. More worth recording is #2193's repositioning of facets: an isolation primitive, not the way to model one user with many chats. Two weeks ago that line in our knowledge base was our inference; today it is the official changelog.

Hermes v0.21.2 answers 'one writer only, everyone else read-only or through the same connection layer'. None of the four root causes was SQLite's fault: profile gateways writing the root database every 5 seconds, the dashboard opening writable at startup, a cron guard doing a raw open() that cancels POSIX locks, and doctor checkpointing under a live holder. The notes call the raw open() 'the classic how to corrupt SQLite recipe'. Our entry's 'WAL corruption on hot update is unresolved' rests on an RFC, and this batch shows that the bulk of the structural problem lives not in the storage engine but in the discipline of bypass writers.

OpenClaw's answer is the most expensive: #144252 spends 47,317 lines solely on 'plugin retirement must not interrupt in-flight work', #145172 moves cold transcripts into immutable JSONL while SQLite keeps identity, and #144454 gives Crabbox and static SSH one shared lease semantics. Together they state one principle: pin the owner before the handover, and if it cannot be pinned, do not hand over.

The cautionary example is ADK 2.9.0: a failed node re-runs on resume instead of replaying, side effects repeat, and the changelog simply demands 'make node bodies idempotent'. That is not a bug; it pushes responsibility back to the caller. For our task queue the day's conclusions are concrete: every step with an external write needs a {run_id}:{step_id} idempotency key; reset carries expectedSessionId; cron carries authProfileId; and not a single SQLite bypass writer may exist. The four prompt-cache fixes across two Claude Code releases tell the other side of the same story: change the replay shape and the cache is gone.
